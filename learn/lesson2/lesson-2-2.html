A First Hand Example

Our first example hand is in the 369 section. On the card it looks like this:

FF 333 666 666 999 (Any 2 suits)

The colors tell us about suit relationships — not which suit to use, but how the suits relate to each other. Let's break it down group by group.

The FF Group (blue)

On the NMJL card, F represents a flower. It's printed in blue because flowers are unsuited tiles. This hand requires 2 flowers.

The 333 and 666 Groups (green)

Both green groups must share a suit — let's call it Suit 1. The 333 means 3 tiles of value 3, and 666 means 3 tiles of value 6. That's 8 of our 14 tiles accounted for.

The 666 and 999 Groups (red)

Red is a different color from green, so these must be a different suit — Suit 2. Three tiles of value 6, and three tiles of value 9. That's all 14.

The Navy Text

"Any 2 suits" confirms that we just need two different suits for the number tiles — we get to choose which ones. And since this is in the 369 section, we know all number tiles must be multiples of 3. Any tile not valued 3, 6, or 9 is useless in this section.
