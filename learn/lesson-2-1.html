Reading the Card

You've figured out the tiles — now let's talk about what winning looks like. In American Mah Jong, players try to complete a legal hand: a set of 14 tiles that matches a pattern on the card.

The legal hands are printed on a card which all players use. The most common one is from the National Mah Jong League (NMJL), updated every year in late March. Each player must have their own card, available through the NMJL's website. The games and exercises in this app are based on NMJL cards going back to 2024.

If you haven't gotten your own card yet, don't worry — I've assembled a mini practice card with a few hands on it, so you can learn without investing in a card of your own.

Sections of the Card

A typical NMJL card has about 75 legal hands, broken down into sections. Each section groups hands that share a common theme. Typical sections include:

  • The Year — tiles that make up the current year (e.g. 2026)
  • 2468 — even tiles
  • Any Like Numbers — groups of the same value across multiple suits
  • Quints — five-of-a-kind hands
  • Consecutive Runs
  • 13579 — odd tiles
  • Winds and Dragons
  • 369 — values that are a multiple of 3
  • Singles and Pairs — hands made entirely of singles or pairs, so jokers cannot be used

Some cards contain other sections in addition to, or instead of, these.

How to Read a Hand

Each Mah Jong hand is written in the same notation, using colors, letters, numbers, and explanatory text. Here are the key rules:

Colors indicate suit relationships. Everything in the same color is the same suit. Different colors mean different suits. Blue does double duty — it marks both non-suited tiles (like flowers or winds) and a third distinct suit when the group contains numbers or dragons.

A group always shares a suit. If you see 333, all three tiles must be the same suit. You cannot mix 3 Bam, 3 Crack, 3 Dot to fill a 333 group.

Always read the navy text. The navy text after each hand provides clarifying information — which suits are allowed, whether numbers can vary, and other details that open up possibilities you might otherwise miss.
