Calling a Tile

How to Call

To call a tile, first announce your intention aloud. Then pick up the tile and place it face up on your rack. Only the most recently discarded tile can be claimed, never older tiles.

But, if the player next in line has already drawn and racked (or tapped) their tile, your call is invalid. You don't get that tile, and play continues as if you never said a thing.

Creating an Exposure

Assuming you successfully called the tile, and it's face up on your rack, add matching tiles from your hand (including jokers) to the called tile to make a complete group. This group is called an exposure. Then complete the action by discarding (and naming) a tile. This ends your turn, and the player to your right executes their turn. This might mean that players have been skipped. This is normal.

Locking an Exposure

Once an exposure is on your rack and the turn has been completed by discarding, that exposure cannot be changed, except to substitute natural tiles for jokers. Until you discard, you can edit the exposure as necessary.

An example: Let's say the hand you are targeting has a group of North winds. A North is discarded, you call it, add 2 North tiles from your hand, and discard. You now have 3 North tiles face up on your rack (a pung in Mah Jong terms). You cannot add a fourth North tile to that group. If you call Mah Jong, the hand you match must have a group of exactly 3 North tiles, no more, no less.

If the hand you are targeting actually requires 4 North winds, a joker can be added to the group at the time of exposure. Now the exposure is 3 natural North tiles, and 1 joker, to make a kong (4 of a kind). Jokers can stand in for any number of tiles in an exposure. It is permissible to claim the North tile, and supplement it with 3 jokers to create a kong. It is even permissible to declare Mah Jong on this hand with NO North tiles, but 4 jokers standing in for them.
