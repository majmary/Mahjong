Discarding

Gameplay starts with the dealer discarding a tile. Unlike most games, however, Mah Jong has some special rules with discards.

The Discard Pile

All discarded tiles are placed face up in the middle of the table so everyone can see them. They are not stacked or obscured in any way. Seeing what has been discarded is important information for every player.

Naming Your Discard

When you discard, you must name your tile accurately. So, you might say six bam, or white dragon. This alerts the other players so they can efficiently check if they would like to pick up that tile. If you make a mistake in naming, don't worry too much, just correct it. A tile isn't actually discarded in Mah Jong until it's properly named.

However — a discarded tile cannot be taken back, even if it hasn't been properly named. Once on the table, it's out of your hand permanently. Naming is what makes it claimable, but the tile itself is gone the moment it leaves your hand.

What Happens Next

Now, one of two things happens. Either the next player to your right draws, or your discard is claimed (also known as called).
