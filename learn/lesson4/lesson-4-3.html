The Blind Pass

The final pass of each Charleston round — first left and second right — can be a blind pass. In a blind pass, you choose fewer than three tiles from your own hand, and use tiles you receive to make up the difference, without looking at them first.

How It Works

Say you only want to pass one tile from your own hand. Here's the sequence:

  • 1Place your one tile face down near the side you're passing to.
  • 2Receive your three tiles from the player passing to you. Do not look at them yet.
  • 3Take two of those received tiles — without looking — and add them to your one tile face down.
  • 4Pass all three face down to the next player.
  • 5Hold the remaining received tile face down until all passing in the round is complete. Then everyone racks their tiles.

If you're passing two tiles from your rack, take one received tile to round it out. If you're passing zero tiles from your rack, pass all three received tiles.

The key rule: the player you are passing to must always receive exactly three tiles. You cannot look at the tiles you receive until all passing in that round is complete.
