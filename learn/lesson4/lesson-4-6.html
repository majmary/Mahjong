Strategy: What to Pass

Step 3: No Clear Direction

Strategy

If I have no power groupings and no pairs — depressingly common — I start looking at overall patterns. Do I have a lot of winds? Dragons? Flowers? Jokers? What's my mix of even tiles vs. odd? Multiples of three? Do I have the same value in all three suits?

The answers point me toward a section: even/odd, Any Like Numbers, Quints, 369, or Winds and Dragons. If nothing points clearly anywhere, I switch from saving tiles to identifying which tiles are least useful. Outliers in value are a good starting point — if I have mostly low numbers and one 9, that 9 is probably not going to help. Winds that don't work together are another — there are hands with North and South, hands with East and West, and hands with all four winds, but no hand uses just North and East.

What Not to Pass

Strategy

In general, I try not to pass flowers in the Charleston. Flowers appear in about half of all legal hands, and even if I can't use them for my target hand, it's better to discard them during play than pass them now. A discarded flower during the game can only be claimed under specific circumstances — in the Charleston, opponents just get them for free.

Every year also has a tile or two that appears in a large number of hands — a "hot" tile. I try not to pass those either, for the same reason. In 2024, it was a 7. Which tile is hot in the current year is covered in the premium Charleston lesson.
