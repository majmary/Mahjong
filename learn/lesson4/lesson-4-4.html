Second Charleston & Courtesy Pass

The Second Charleston

After the first Charleston, a second round typically follows. The direction of passing reverses:

Second left → Second across → Second right

The final pass (second right) can be a blind pass, just like first left.

The Veto

Only the first Charleston is mandatory. Any player can veto the second Charleston — and if one player vetoes, no one gets to do it.

Strategy

I almost never veto the second Charleston. It's a chance to see nine more tiles — they might help my hand, or at least tell me what other players started with. The only time I veto is when I'm already close to Mah Jong — about ten or more tiles matching a hand — because I don't want to risk giving away tiles I need. That situation almost never happens after the deal.

The Courtesy Pass

After all Charlestons are complete, you and the player across from you agree on how many tiles to exchange — anywhere from zero to three. Once all players have completed the courtesy pass, the game begins.

You should have a full rack of tiles: 13 if you didn't deal, 14 if you did. The dealer discards one tile without drawing first, and Mah Jong is underway.
