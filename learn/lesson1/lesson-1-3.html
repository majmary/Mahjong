Dots & Dragons

Dots

Dots are dots because, well, they have dots on their faces. In this suit, the dots are often multicolored, and the 1 dot often has a large circular icon. It can be easily confused with a joker in one of the most common tile face designs. If you end up with a set that has no Western numbers, just count the dots on the tile to figure out its value.

In my game, dots are blue — because the traditional Mah Jong colors are red, green and blue, and red and green were already taken.

Dragons

There are three dragons in American Mah Jong: red, green and white. Traditionally the red and green dragons are depicted as a stylized dragon, while the white dragon is an open frame. That open frame is the inspiration behind its alternate name, soap — it looks like a bar of soap. It's also why this tile is used as a 0 in NMJL notation, which we'll cover in the next lesson.

Each dragon is associated with a suit: the red dragon goes with Cracks, the green dragon with Bams, and the white dragon with Dots.

In my game, the dragons are a darker version of their suit color, with their initial displayed. The white dragon shows a 0 instead of a W — that helps distinguish it from the West wind tile, and reminds us of its role as a 0 on the card.
